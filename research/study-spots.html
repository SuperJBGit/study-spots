Things that change the list

  • Library hours come from a public data feed. U of T Libraries publishes its hours through LibCal as open JSON, 12 weeks ahead, for 63 libraries and service desks. A website can read it directly, so the app never has to type in library hours. See Hours feed.
  • No library extends its hours for December exams in the feed as of Oct 12. Robarts Common is already 24 hours Monday to Thursday, and a few small libraries shorten their hours late in exams.
  • Only Arts & Science students belong to a college. Engineering, Music, Kinesiology and Architecture students belong to their faculty instead, so the college question only applies to Arts & Science.
  • Libraries publish their entry rule; nothing else does. The Libraries & hours page tags every library with an access label and defines them on hover: Public means open to all during posted hours, U of T access means a TCard is required for entry, Restricted means you may need an appointment or access card. Outside the libraries, U of T says most building doors are on staff fobs, so most “members only” rooms aren’t locked. Some do swipe: the ECE Study Hall, Bahen’s CS labs after hours, and Civil’s fob-access common room.
  • Innis College Library is closed until late spring 2027. Innis students still have their own lounge, carrels and commuter room.
  • Hart House is mid-renovation (first phase runs to spring 2027) but stays open. Its hours page looks out of date.
  • Arts & Science and Engineering share a fall reading week, Oct 26–30, and the university is closed for Thanksgiving on Oct 12. The count plan avoids both.

Access model

How access works at U of T

What decides who can use a spot

  • Library access label. Public: open to all during posted hours. U of T access: TCard required for entry (Robarts, Gerstein, OISE, ECSL, Earth Sciences, Math and a few specialist libraries). Restricted: may need an appointment or access card (Law, Dentistry, Massey, ROM).
  • Faculty. Engineering has one common room per program, plus the Pit for everyone. Medicine has MD-only spaces.
  • College, Arts & Science only. Lounges, carrels and event rooms are for that college’s students.
  • Where you live. Several colleges’ best rooms are commuter lounges for members who live off campus (UC, Vic, St. Mike’s, Innis). New College opens its amenity rooms to its residents too, whatever their faculty.
  • Courses. Bahen’s CS labs open to anyone in a CS program or taking at least one CSC course, so the rule depends on enrolment, not program.
  • Time of day. Bahen is open to all during business hours but CS-only after. Several spots change hours or rules in exam season.

What the app should ask judgement

  1. Faculty, and program only if Engineering.
  2. College, asked only for Arts & Science.
  3. Where you live (a residence or off campus). This gives commuter status without a separate question.
  4. Optional: “Taking a CSC course?” It only matters for Bahen’s labs.

Show members-only spots only to members. Most aren’t locked, so a non-member could walk in, but sending outsiders into “for our students” rooms is the fastest way to get colleges and clubs to complain about the app.

Where the access data is weak

  • Engineering common-room locations come from the student wiki and club pages. Some date from 2021–23, so confirm they haven’t moved.
  • Most college pages say “for our students” but name no door rule or hours. Those rows are marked assumed or scout.
  • Faculties beyond Engineering, Medicine and the colleges (Rotman, Law, Music, Daniels, Pharmacy, Kinesiology) aren’t covered yet. Each probably has its own student lounges.
  • Access facts change rarely, which makes them a good thing to crowdsource with a “Couldn’t get in?” button, unlike busyness. A few reports a term would keep them current.

Data source

Hours feed

The Libraries & hours page and U of T’s LibCal hours page show the same hours. LibCal also serves them as public JSON that any website can read, because it allows calls from any domain. I tested it on Oct 12.

GET https://libcal.library.utoronto.ca/api_hours_grid.php?iid=478&format=json&weeks=12   (dated hours, up to 12 weeks)
GET https://libcal.library.utoronto.ca/api_hours_today.php?iid=478&lid=0&format=json   (today, with open/closed status)

What it gives you

  • 63 locations: 45 libraries plus service desks and sub-spaces such as Robarts Common’s building hours.
  • Hours for each date, so holidays, reading week and term changes come through automatically. Pratt, for example, switches to longer weekend hours on Oct 30.
  • A “currently open” flag for today.

What it doesn’t

  • Holidays are missing for some libraries. Kelly, Knox, Math, Earth Sciences and Art show no Thanksgiving closure and no winter closure, and ECSL shows itself open on Thanksgiving. The app should treat university closure days (Thanksgiving, Dec 24–Jan 5) as closed unless a library says otherwise.
  • No map coordinates (the fields are empty), no access rules, no noise levels. Those stay in the spot list.
  • Nothing outside the libraries: Myhal, Hart House, Student Commons and the colleges’ lounges aren’t in it.
  • The feed isn’t documented for outside use judgement. Cache it once a day rather than calling it for every visitor, and ask U of T Libraries before launch.

Headcount plan

Three weeks of counts, two questions

The counts test whether the app would have anything useful to say before you build it.

1. Predictability. Can one week of counts predict the next two, at the level of “plenty of seats / some / full”? If yes, a predicted-busyness feature works without anyone reporting anything.

2. Imbalance. At the same moment, is one spot full while another nearby has plenty of seats? If everything fills and empties together, there’s nothing to redirect people to, and a static guide would be enough.

When

Mon Oct 5 – Fri Oct 9Baseline10 rounds, 2 per weekday. The first round also scouts each spot (see below).
Tue Oct 13 – Fri Oct 16Test 16 rounds. Mon Oct 12 is Thanksgiving and the university is closed.
Mon Oct 19 – Fri Oct 23Test 26 rounds. Midterms pile up the week before reading week, so this tests whether the pattern holds when campus is busier.
Mon Oct 26 – Fri Oct 30Reading weekNo counts. It isn’t a normal week, for Arts & Science or Engineering.

Route: 8 spots, one loop

I picked the south end of campus so a round fits between classes: big libraries, engineering spaces and one niche quiet room, all close together. Every spot on the route is open to any U of T student, so the counts describe spots the app would show everyone. A round should take about 20–25 minutes estimate. Walk them in this order.

    Backup if the Clark Reading Room turns out not to be open to everyone: the Donnelly Centre’s Bamboo Forest atrium (160 College St). Optional second route if a friend helps: Robarts Common, Sidney Smith Commons, Kelly Library 2nd floor, Pratt Library and OISE Library ground floor.

    Time slots

    SlotWindowWeek 1 targetWhy
    Morning9:00–11:002–3 roundsSpots fill up as the first classes end.
    Midday11:00–14:003 roundsLunch peak. This is where imbalance should show if it exists.
    Afternoon14:00–17:003 roundsThe longest stretch of studying between classes.
    Evening18:00–21:002 roundsOnly the libraries, the Pit and Myhal stay open late. Sid Smith Commons closes at 7:30.

    Start every round at the same minute past the hour (say :20), so you aren’t catching the rush between classes in some rounds and not others. Weeks 2 and 3 should repeat Week 1’s mix of slots and days as closely as you can, because predictions only get tested where Week 1 has data.

    First visit: scout each spot

    • Pick a fixed count zone you can count in under 2 minutes (a whole room, or one floor of a big one). Write down where it starts and ends.
    • Count capacity: chairs at tables, plus one per couch cushion.
    • Note outlets (most seats / some / none), food rules from signs, noise, and whether any door needed a TCard.
    • Take one photo of the space with no faces in it. The app will need photos later.

    Each count

    • A seat is taken if a person or their stuff is on it or in front of it.
    • Record occupied seats and the time you counted that spot, not just when the round started.
    • If an event, a class or a closure is affecting the space, note it and leave the level blank.
    • Don’t round. Write the number you counted.

    Levels

    Plenty: under 50% taken Some: 50–85% Full: over 85%, or no free seat in sight

    The cut-offs are my choice judgement. 85% is about when a student would give up and go somewhere else.

    Log

    One row per spot per round, in a spreadsheet on your phone. Use the IDs from the route above so the counts join back to the spot list.

    date,round_start,spot_id,time_counted,occupied,capacity,note

    How to score it

    Predictions

    • For each spot and time slot, the prediction is the average % taken in Week 1. If Week 1 has no count for that slot, use the spot’s overall Week 1 average.
    • Turn each prediction into a level, then compare it with every Week 2 and Week 3 count.
    • Comparison: also predict every count from the spot’s overall Week 1 average, ignoring the time of day. If the time-of-day version isn’t clearly better, the time pattern isn’t adding anything.

    Pass marks judgement

    • At least 70% of predictions hit the right level.
    • At most 10% are bad misses: predicted plenty or some, but it was full. A bad miss sends someone to a full room, which is the mistake that loses users.
    • The time-of-day version beats the comparison by about 10 points or more.
    • Imbalance: in at least half of the midday rounds, one spot is full while another on the route has plenty.
    ResultWhat it means for the app
    Predictable and imbalancedBuild it: spot info plus predicted busyness. The idea holds up.
    Predictable but balancedA good static guide does the job. Predictions don’t send anyone anywhere better.
    Not predictableBusyness needs live data (UofT Wi-Fi counts or student reports), which is the hard path. Ask UofT IT about Wi-Fi data before building anything.

    What three weeks can’t tell you

    • Exam season (Dec 10–22) is busier than anything in October, so expect October numbers to under-predict December.
    • One counter on one route covers about 8 spots. That’s enough to test the method, not to cover campus.
    • Weekends, Winter term and members-only rooms are untested.
    • Effort: about 22 rounds × 20–25 minutes, or roughly 7–9 hours over three weeks estimate. Splitting routes with a friend doubles coverage for the same time each.
